Chart dictionary.

Charts are the system’s only imagery, so they follow the same rules as type: grey structure, one accent signal, numbers you can read. This page holds the chart palettes, the anatomy every chart shares, six fully specified basic charts and the full dictionary.

01-B
Colour roles

Seven roles. A colour has exactly one.

A palette is a tool; a role is a meaning. Highlight says look here. Alert says act. Variance says good or bad against a scenario. They never share a mark.

01-B-01
Sequential

Ordered from low to high.

One hue; lightness carries the order. Blue by default, vermilion only when high is bad.

01-B-02
Diverging

Two sequential arms around a neutral midpoint.

Only when the midpoint means something: zero, target, parity. Both arms ramp in lightness at the same rate.

01-B-03
Categorical · hue

Distinct colours for unordered groups.

Storytelling only. Slots in fixed order. 1–3 categories pass all three colour-vision deficiencies but not greyscale, so always label directly. 4–6 are weak for colour-blind readers. 7+ go to Other.

01-B-04
Categorical · grey

Parts of a whole in IBCS reports.

Four steps at most, dark to light from the baseline, 1px surface gaps. A fifth part goes to Other. Colour stays free for meaning.

01-B-05
Highlight

Directs the eye to the message.

No judgement, one per chart. Blue in IBCS notation; vermilion in classic, where it collides with alert.

01-B-06
Alert

Something is wrong and needs action.

▲

Vermilion plus the ▲ glyph, never colour alone. Reserved for thresholds and status, not for an ordinary bad variance.

01-B-07
Variance

Good or bad against PY, PL or FC.

IBCS only. Jade for good, vermilion for bad, by business meaning. Position and the ± sign carry it too.

01-C
Vision check

Measured, not assumed.

Each cell shows the set after simulation (Machado 2009, full severity) and the smallest colour difference inside it, ΔE CIE76. House thresholds for sets: ● 40+ pass, ◐ 20–39 weak, ○ under 20 fail. Ramp rows measure the smallest step in lightness between neighbours instead: ● 6+, ◐ 3–5, ○ under 3 or inverted. Values follow the current theme, notation and variance setting. The Vision setting in Tweaks applies the same simulation to the whole page.

02 · ANATOMY

Every chart, one anatomy.

Eight rules that hold for every entry below, plus four from IBCS for scenarios and variances. A chart type only adds rules of its own when these aren’t enough.

02-01

Title is the finding. The subtitle names the measure, the unit and the period.

02-01

Title block. The message is the title. Below it: entity, then measure, unit, period and scenarios, e.g. AC vs PY.
02-02

One accent. It goes on the mark the title is about. All other marks are ink-2 or mid.

02-02

Highlight is not a scenario. One highlight colour, blue by default, marks the message. It never stands for AC, PY, PL, FC or a variance.
02-03

Label directly. Put names at line ends and values on bars. Use a legend only past three series.

02-04

Gridlines recede. Gridlines only on the value axis, 1px surface-3. The zero baseline is ink.

02-04

No gridlines when labelled. If every mark carries its value, drop the gridlines and the value axis. Keep them only for unlabelled lines and scatters.
02-05

Length starts at zero. Bars, columns and areas include zero. Lines and dots may crop the axis.

02-05

Zero, or mark the break. Axes start at zero. A cropped axis shows a break mark at its foot. Charts side by side share one scale.
02-06

Ticks are spec. Tick labels are BKribitz Mono 12 in ink-3, with tabular figures, set in CSS pixels so they never scale down. Few ticks, round numbers.

02-07

Sort by value. Categories go in value order unless they have a natural one, such as time, rank or bins.

02-08

Source and n, always. One mono line under the figure gives the source and the sample size.

02-09

Scenario notation. AC solid ink-2, PY solid mid, PL as an outline, FC hatched at 45°. The same in every chart.

02-10

Variance colour. Emerald for a good variance, vermilion for a bad one, by business meaning, not by sign. Neither is used for anything else.

02-11

Time across, structure down. Periods run left to right as columns or lines. Categories run top to bottom as bars.

02-12

Variances are explicit. Show ΔPY or ΔPL as variance bars or pins, never only as the gap between two marks.

03-01 · COMPARISON

Use when
Comparing one measure across 3–15 categories, especially when the names are long.
Avoid when
The categories are time periods (use a column or line chart), or there are more than about 15.
Encoding
length → value · position y → category
Rules
Sort descending. Bar thickness 60% of the band. Put the value at the bar’s end and drop the axis if every bar is labelled.Structure runs top to bottom, sorted descending. Bars are AC in ink-2 at 60% of the band. Every bar is labelled, so there is no value axis or gridline. The highlight is blue.

Rules
Bins touch, with a 1px surface gap and no wider spacing. State the bin width in the subtitle. Mark the median with an accent rule.
