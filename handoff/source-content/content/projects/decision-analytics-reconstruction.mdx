<Lead>
The 2018 Paraguay presidential election, reconstructed as three connected decision modules
(voter segmentation, budget allocation and poll tracking) from what a campaign could see in
early 2018.
</Lead>

Given only eight public polls, census aggregates and a fixed budget, how do you track a national
race, allocate money across the country, and stay honest about what you actually know? The
verified result the reconstruction reasons toward is public: Mario Abdo Benítez won by 3.70
percentage points (46.43% against 42.73%) on 61.25% turnout, according to the electoral
tribunal's (TSJE) final results.

<Marker num="01" label="Context" id="sec-01" />

The public anchors are verified: the TSJE returns, DGEEC census aggregates, the INE 2018
household survey on internet and WhatsApp use, and eight published tracking polls. Every
individual voter is synthetic. A 50,000-record voter file is raked to the verified
department-level anchors (turnout, gender, rural share, language), so no real person is
represented while the aggregates still hold.

Each artifact carries an epistemic tag (verified, calibrated, simulated or illustrative), and
the register of those tags is part of the repository. The tag travels with the number.

<Marker num="02" label="Process" id="sec-02" />

**Module A: segmentation and turnout.** K-means with six segments on the synthetic population,
plus a Platt-calibrated turnout propensity. Silhouette 0.2562 against a gate of 0.22, bootstrap
ARI 0.4304 against a gate of 0.40, Brier score 0.1185 on a 15,000-voter holdout. The model also
reaches an AUC of about 0.89, which is deliberately not headlined: the propensity features share
calibration anchors with the target, so the AUC measures how well the raking was recovered, not
how well the model generalises.

**Module B: allocation.** A mixed-integer linear program (PuLP with CBC) over 2,772 rows:
18 departments × 11 channels × 14 weeks, under a budget, reach caps, an exchange-rate layer
and an 80% coverage floor. It maximises a linearised persuasion proxy and is served through a
FastAPI application.

**Module C: poll tracking.** A Gaussian random walk with house effects, fitted in PyMC to the
eight polls, then stratified Monte Carlo scenarios (10,000 draws by default). The tracking curve
is a retrodiction: it conditions on the verified outcome, so it cannot be read as a forecast.

The three modules run as one pipeline, A to B to C, with an enforced handoff of the allocation
between them.

<Marker num="03" label="Results" id="sec-03" />

On a USD 6M envelope, the MILP produces about 54.77% more linearised persuasion-proxy contacts
than a department-uniform allocation. That is a solver comparison on synthetic reach, not a
historical effect of the 2018 campaign.

The one true out-of-sample check is reported unflattered. Refitting the tracker with each real
poll wave held out, and the election result provably excluded, the 95% band caught the held-out
poll in 3 of 6 tested waves, with a Brier score of 0.31 on the sign of the margin. Eight polls
are far too few to claim forecasting skill, and the project does not claim it.

The reconstruction does not claim that analytics caused the 3.70-point margin. The public result
is the anchor; showing what the model can and cannot support is the point of the work.

What this does not establish:

- A causal effect. A with-and-without-analytics counterfactual cannot be verified here.
- Individual behaviour. Aggregate calibration of synthetic voters does not mean real behaviour
  was recovered.
- Campaign economics. Module B optimises linearised contacts, not audited 2018 campaign
  accounting or fitted response curves.
- Full scale. The pipeline runs 50,000 voters over 14 weeks; the 4.26M-voter roll is a design
  reference.

Functional: the three modules run end to end, and every headline number lives in one numeric
source-of-truth table that CI checks. It is a portfolio reconstruction, not a production campaign
system. Code, the case study and the validation gates:
[github.com/RafaelBraga-Kribitz/decision-analytics-reconstruction](https://github.com/RafaelBraga-Kribitz/decision-analytics-reconstruction).
