<Lead>
A total-cost-of-ownership model for deploying humanoid robots in intralogistics, capturing
capex, energy, maintenance, and labor offset to find where a humanoid fleet beats conventional
automation. <strong>Numbers below are placeholders</strong> pending the owner's figures.
</Lead>

<Marker num="01" label="Context" id="sec-01" />

<Node num="01-01" title="Introduction">
Warehouse operators face a choice between fixed conventional automation, conveyors, AS/RS,
fixed arms, and general-purpose humanoid robots that flex across tasks. The question is not
capability but <strong>cost over time</strong>: at what horizon, and under what duty cycle,
does a humanoid fleet become the cheaper option?

This brief frames that decision as a total-cost-of-ownership comparison over a five-year
horizon, isolating the assumptions that actually move the breakeven point.
</Node>

<Node
  num="01-01-01"
  level={3}
  title="Scope boundary"
  noteRef="1"
  note="Holding shared costs constant isolates the humanoid-vs-conventional delta from facility noise."
>
The model covers direct deployment cost only. Facility retrofits, software licensing, and
integration labor are held constant across both fleets and excluded from the
comparison.<NoteRef n="1" />
</Node>

<Node
  num="01-02"
  title="Approach / Key Assumptions"
  noteRef="2"
  note="Power draw is the single most sensitive input. A ±0.4 kW shift moves breakeven by roughly a quarter."
>
The model sums four cost streams per deployment: <strong>capex + deployment</strong>,
<strong>energy</strong> (driven by power draw, here assumed 2.4 kW continuous per
unit)<NoteRef n="2" />, <strong>maintenance</strong>, and the <strong>labor offset</strong>
each unit displaces. Conventional automation carries lower upfront cost but higher fixed run
cost; humanoids invert that profile.
</Node>

<Marker num="02" label="Process" id="sec-02" />

<Node num="02-01" title="Architecture">
Each cost stream feeds a single TCO model, simulated across the five-year horizon to produce a
cumulative cost curve and the breakeven point against the conventional baseline.
</Node>

<Node
  num="02-02"
  title="Key Decisions"
  noteRef="3"
  note="Run-in heads are content L2: bold, in the reading column, never numbered. Compare 01-01-01 above, which is numbered because it is addressable."
>
<RunIn>Horizon.</RunIn> Five years matches the expected service life of a humanoid unit before
major refurbishment.

<RunIn>Duty cycle.</RunIn> Continuous power draw is the conservative assumption; intermittent
operation pushes breakeven earlier.

<RunIn>Labor offset.</RunIn> Modeled per shift displaced, the single most sensitive input.
Small changes here move breakeven by quarters.<NoteRef n="3" />
</Node>

<Marker num="03" label="Results" id="sec-03" />

<Node num="03-01" title="Outcome">
Under the placeholder assumptions, the humanoid fleet starts more expensive on day one but its
flatter run cost overtakes conventional automation at roughly <strong>year 2.5</strong>, the
breakeven below. Past that point, every additional month favors the humanoid deployment.
</Node>
